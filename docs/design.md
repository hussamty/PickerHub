# PickerHub desktop interface

## Reference review

Four interaction studies set the direction of this redesign. They are research material; nothing from them is copied.

- A liquid interface: circular actions that swell under the pointer, flow into their neighbours, squash when pressed, and name themselves in a pill tooltip; menus that grow out of the control that opens them. https://x.com/koppkev/status/2120471771602092136
- A spine menu: a pane of frosted glass attached to the window edge through concave shoulders, opening into a panel where a highlight follows the pointer from row to row. https://x.com/seanaiux/status/2120222459110789574
- A specimen card deck: a fanned pile of frosted cards, each flipping to a reverse side of measurements, with a small pager below. https://x.com/sanyampunia/status/2120080276022051218
- A light and dark switch built as one physical object: a frosted knob in a recessed track, a pixel moon that bursts and gathers again as a sun, a rim light that travels round the knob, and a drag that scrubs the change. https://www.scrolltide.co/#c-ember-tide

Earlier research into PowerToys Color Picker, Sip, ColorSlurp, and Just Color Picker still informs the feature set: one direct pick action, copy controls next to each format, and palettes as a primary part of the app.

## Design

The brief sets design variance to 7, motion intensity to 6, and visual density to 4.

- Navigation is a spine grown out of the right edge. At rest it is a 58-pixel rail of icons, so every tool stays one click away; under the pointer or keyboard focus it opens to 236 pixels over the page and shows labels beneath a Menu header, which is a title, not a control. A glass pill follows the pointer between rows and squashes on press; a yellow pill marks the current tool and slides when the tool changes.
- Icon actions are liquid buttons: 40-pixel drops that widen on hover with an overshoot and flatten on press. Grouped actions share one fluid body, so a swelling button flows into its neighbours. Each has a tooltip and an accessible name.
- Palette grid/list cards and history deck fronts are filled edge to edge with their exact color, without a tint or a central disc. Labels use the contrast engine’s black/white ink; the reverse measurement face retains the shared glass material. Hovering opens the fan, clicking or Space turns the top card over to lightness, HEX, RGB, HSL, CMYK, and its tonal row. Arrow keys and the pager step through the pile, and a sheet of dots beside the deck jumps to any color.
- The theme switch sits in the header. Clicking plays the change; dragging the knob scrubs it. The room behind the glass follows the knob continuously and the theme itself turns at the midpoint.
- Everything that opens over the page is in-window glass with one motion: settings, prompts and confirmations are sheets over a dimmed page, and select buttons drop a glass list that grows out of the button. No separate system windows or stock drop-downs remain.
- Pages fit their viewport: the title and its description share one row, the content column is capped at 1300 pixels, and the color page shows every tool without scrolling at the default window size. The smallest window is 900 × 640; there every page fits its width, the top bar keeps every action whole, and the navigation rail shrinks its rows (down to 30 pixels) rather than cut any off.
- Palette of the day is a sealed envelope of brand-yellow paper, leaning toward the pointer in 3D. Its geometry is exact: one 10-pixel radius for every outer corner, a V-shaped mouth with a filleted point, and straight flap edges meeting in a filleted tip, on which sits a brand-black seal with the PickerHub mark. A soft specular band with a faint pearl tint follows the light; the lip catches a highlight, the flap casts a real blurred shadow, and the name is debossed. Opening it is the one orchestrated moment in the app: the flap turns in perspective onto a pinstriped charcoal lining (its corners squaring off past upright, so the fold never pinches), the cards are pushed out into a fan, confetti bursts from the mouth, the envelope sinks and fades within its stage, and the fan opens into a row. Afterwards the page is quiet: the seven role cards in a shallow arc, each lifting and tilting under the pointer, and one pane beneath with the story, the countdown and the actions. `--preview-envelope` opens it in a temporary library to replay at will. Reference for the opening: https://x.com/insporadesign/status/2120404687291584780
- The color page opens on a specimen: the selected color at full size, labelled in the ink the Rust contrast engine picks for it.
- Image analysis shows each color where it is. The picture sits in a frame of frosted glass; a small glass disc marks every color, with that color's pixel framed in white and black at its centre so it shows on any ground, and a leader runs from each mark out to a glass label with the color's name, code and share. The engine segments the picture by nearest color (OKLab, smoothed by a 3x3 majority pass) and marks each color at the point of its largest region farthest from the region's edge, so a mark always sits deep inside its color. Labels stand in evenly spaced columns beside the frame: the left half of the marks is labeled on the left and the right half on the right, marks are matched to slots by least total leader length, which leaves no two straight leaders crossing, and a leader that would graze another mark bends once round it. Leaders draw outward one after another, largest color first, each over 1.1 seconds on an ease-in-out curve and 140 milliseconds apart; the arrowhead grows in over the last stretch and each label settles as its leader arrives. Reduced motion shows them at rest. Leaders use the theme's ink with a halo of its opposite, so they read on any picture in either theme. Below 780 pixels the labels stand in one column after the picture in reading order, and below 560 they keep only the swatch, code and share. Pointing at a label lights its leader and mark; clicking it opens the color's actions, which copy the color's code in the format chosen in Settings or send it to Contrast Check. A picture with more than five colors opens in compact view: only its five most dominant colors are marked and labeled, and a Show all toggle beneath the picture brings back the rest, then offers Show top 5 to return. The guide redraws in place with the same leader motion, so the page keeps its scroll position. Save palette and export take the colors on show. Shades that look alike count as one color and small distinct accents are kept, so the list reads as a person would describe the picture.
- Color names come from one perceptual classifier in the engine, and every name in the app uses it: the editor, the library and palettes, the image guide, sampled pixels and the daily cards. It reads a color in OKLCH. A color is neutral only when its chroma is genuinely low (under 0.02, rising to 0.05 near black and 0.04 near white), and is then named by lightness alone: black, charcoal, dark gray, gray, silver, light gray, white. A faint tint gives a tinted gray named by its tint and lightness (purple gray, lavender gray, slate gray, taupe), so a muted purple is never plain gray. In the dark, where a little chroma shows plainly, that limit asks for the same saturation (chroma against lightness) instead, so a dark brown door or an eggplant is named brown or purple rather than a tinted gray. Anything stronger takes one of eleven hue families from fixed OKLCH hue bands, with brown for darker, softer oranges and reds, pink for pale reds, purple for dark, dull magentas, and magenta for bright, strong purples. Its name is the nearest of 128 references in OKLab among its family and, within 8 degrees of a band edge or close to a lightness or chroma threshold, the neighbouring family, so a name changes only where the color does. Image colors are named from each group's weighted OKLab median, snapped to a shade that occurs in the picture, so shadows, highlights, compression fringes and stray pixels pull neither the color nor its name; pixels under 16 of 255 opacity are not counted. Libraries and palette files from 1.0 keep the names people typed, and swatches that still carry their old automatic name take the new one.
- The eyedropper lens is a block of brand black: magnified pixels in a rounded well, the chosen pixel framed in yellow between two black lines so it shows on any color, and the value beneath. It refreshes every frame and its zoom glides between levels.

## System

Tokens and control states live in desktop/Theme.axaml. Glass, the fluid button body, and the backdrop are in desktop/Glass.cs; the theme switch is in desktop/ThemeSwitch.cs. Controls are built in C# and carry style classes.

- Color: the TrineHub palette. Black #111111, yellow #FFF000, white #FFFFFF, and nothing else in the chrome, so samples read true. Dark is the home theme: black room, white ink, yellow for the one filled action, the current tool, and focus. Light inverts black and white and keeps yellow fixed, as the brand's reverse rule does for the logo. Yellow never appears as text or a thin mark on a light ground: yellow fills always carry black ink (17.5:1), and where the dark theme writes in yellow (the H of the wordmark, pass marks, focus rings, slider and checkbox accents) the light theme writes in black.
- Backdrop: brand black or its light inverse with three pools of light. Each pool fades along an eased curve of several stops rather than one linear ramp, which keeps wide faint gradients free of visible steps on 8-bit screens. There is no texture or grain over the room or the glass.
- Glass: one material for everything that floats, defined once in ui/src/app.css (Glass system): a translucent tint per layer (panes, sheets and menus, dense strips), the same sheen from the top-left corner, a 1-pixel rim in the theme's rim color with a lit top edge, one blur (18 pixels, saturation 150 percent) and two shadow sizes. Surfaces that open over other content (the spine, sheets, menus) always blur what is behind them; with full glass the large panes do too. The spine is one body of that glass, its concave shoulders included: the tint, sheen and blur are clipped to its outline, its shadow is cast by the same outline, and one rim runs all the way round. Accent fills carry the emphasis and cast only the neutral glass shadow, never a colored glow. Layers stack in a fixed order: spine, menus, scrim, sheets, toasts, cursor.
- Mark and icon: an eyedropper written as one broad-nib stroke in the shape of a P. The stroke leaves a rounded rubber bulb through a slim neck where the bowl meets the stem, sweeps along the bottom of the bowl and round a superellipse to the top, turns down into the stem and narrows, with no corner where the taper begins, into the dropper's fine tip; a drop of the picked colour, white, falls from it. A pen held at a slight angle sets the width throughout, so verticals are full and horizontals lighter and the change between them is continuous. The bowl is the eyedropper's lens: the pixels under it show through, the picked one white at the centre. Flat brand yellow and white on a brand-black rounded tile. scripts/mark.py generates desktop/Assets/icon.svg; scripts/icons.py renders every size and, at 64 pixels and below, draws the mark 10 percent larger with the picked pixel alone in the lens. The envelope's seal reads the same source.
- Shape: interactive controls are pills, glass panes use 24 to 26-pixel corners, inputs and swatches 12 to 14.
- Spacing: a 4-pixel rhythm. Panes are padded 24 by 22 pixels, page sections are 20 pixels apart and the parts inside them 12, and a page title sits 20 pixels above its first section.
- Type: Plus Jakarta Sans sets Latin and Noto Kufi Arabic sets Arabic. Both are geometric and low in contrast and share a 536-unit x-height, so a line that mixes them reads as one face, with Plus Jakarta Sans giving English a crisp, high-end voice. One scale serves both scripts, 11/12.5/13/14/15/17/20/24/28 pixels (caption, label, control, body, lead, title, heading, feature, display). Headings are SemiBold, controls Medium, and the wordmark Bold. Arabic is set at 1.7 line height and Latin at 1.5; Latin headings from 20 pixels are tracked in by 1.2 percent, and Arabic is never letter-spaced, because spacing would break its joins. Codes and measurements use the system monospace face, always left to right.
- Direction: Arabic reads from the right and English from the left, and every layout follows: page content and its heading, dialogs, the error banner, the toast, the specimen (name at the start, code and actions at the end), the daily cards (first role at the start, labels aligned to it), codes in lists, and arrows for back and forward. Direction is set on the surfaces that hold text and inherited by their children, so a change of language in Settings turns everything at once. Names and codes set inside a sentence are wrapped in Unicode isolates, so their own direction never reorders the words around them. Surfaces laid out left to right on purpose (the title bar, the card stage, the specimen face) name the reading start per language instead of mirroring.
- Toasts: status news never sits in the top bar. It rises as a toast from the bottom corner where reading starts (bottom-right in Arabic, clear of the navigation rail; bottom-left in English): a small pane of the shared frosted glass with a lit rim, 18-pixel corners and a soft shadow, holding one line of Medium 13-pixel text and, at its reading start, a yellow signal dot, or a swatch when the message names a color. It arrives with a short rise and overshoot, takes fresh news with a small nod, and leaves on its own after three to six seconds depending on its length; pointing at it holds it and clicking it sends it away. Reduced motion keeps only the fade. Screen readers hear every message as a polite announcement.
- Icons: Tabler outline icons drawn in the inherited text color, so they follow their button state.
- Motion: every movement reports a state change (hover, press, page change, card step, card flip, theme change). Liquid motion means an overshoot on release and a squash on press. Responses start on the frame of the input and finish within 300 ms: the tool marker moves before the page is built, and the theme turns by restyling what is on screen, without rebuilding it. The "تقليل الحركة" setting removes all travel and keeps the state changes.
- Cursor: a hand drawn by the interface replaces the system cursor while a mouse is in use (ui/src/components/HandCursor.svelte). It is brand black with a sheen and a white rim, so it reads on either theme and on any sampled color, and yellow appears only on the black: the lit fingertip, the thin rim at rest, the precision ring between two black lines. At rest it leans back; over anything clickable it points upright and grows 8 percent; a press shortens the finger, shrinks to 90 percent and sends a ring out from the click; over a slider or a dragged handle it opens and closes into a fist while dragging; over a surface that picks a pixel it steps aside and a hollow ring marks the point, so the pixel stays in view. Every pose turns and scales about the index fingertip, which is the exact click point. Elements ask for a pose with the inherited `--hand` property (set by role in app.css); text fields keep the system I-beam, and touch, pens and forced colors keep the system cursor. The lens window and the Wayland overlay keep their own crosshair.
- Tooltips use in-window popup overlays and the shared SheetTint glass, rim and reduced-transparency fallback.
- Linux and Windows default to a branded glass titlebar; fixed window controls do not deform. The top bar is one 56-pixel glass row read in order: the mark and wordmark, the eyedropper (the one filled action), then, after free space to move the window by, undo and redo, the theme switch, a hairline, and the window actions. Undo and redo use curved arrows and mirror with the language, so undo always points back along the reading direction. Window actions are borderless and tint under the pointer, so they stay quieter than the app's own controls. Empty bar space supports thresholded native dragging and double-click maximize/restore, with six-pixel resize targets in the normal state. The middle window action answers the current size: maximize from a normal window, restore (two overlapping windows) from a maximized one. Minimize keeps its own slot and only ever minimizes. A narrowing window drops the wordmark below a 640-pixel bar, and never an action. macOS retains system window controls.
- The shell uses a bounded content row, not a vertically unbounded dock. Bottom spacing belongs to the scrollable content, so the final action stays reachable. Scrollbars have a stable 14-pixel track and rounded thumb; scroll position is never animated.
- Image Analysis accepts one dropped image file, clipboard image pixels or one copied image file. Text editing keeps normal paste behavior; modal dialogs and active imports suppress image paste. File size and pixel limits apply to the shared import pipeline.

Sidebar opening and closing share one 460 ms cubic ease-in-out transition. The same transition survives direction reversal. Pointer exit has a 160 ms grace period, cancelled on re-entry. Reduced motion uses immediate state changes. The mark uses the small-size artwork at 128 pixels in the tray and top bar to stay crisp at high DPI, multi-resolution ICO for Windows, ICNS for macOS, and eight hicolor PNG sizes for Linux.
